01
Structure · The Cosmic Web
A web without edges
Scientific fact
Dark matter and galaxies form a web-like structure spanning the entire universe — filaments connect nodes, nodes are galaxy clusters, and voids are the enclosed empty regions between them.
The universe is not a scatter of isolated islands, but a web with no border.
We stand at one node, mistaking it for the whole — when it is only one crossing among countless connections.
Every filament is taut. Every void breathes.
02
Structure · Topological forms
Cluster, loop, void
Scientific fact
The topology of the universe takes three basic forms: clusters (connected components), loops (filaments and tunnels), and voids (cavities and shells).
Three forms: gather, connect, empty. Gathered into stars, connected into filaments, emptied into shells.
A void is not an absence — it is another way of existing.
A loop is not a closure — it is a tunnel to somewhere else.
08
Structure · Multi-scale
A nested universe
Scientific fact
Cosmic structure is multi-scale: small clusters nest inside filaments, filaments inside walls, walls inside voids.
Inside the void there are walls. On the walls, filaments. On the filaments, clusters. Inside the clusters, smaller voids.
The universe is a set of Russian dolls — no innermost, no outermost.
03
Persistence · Betti curves
The heartbeat of the universe
Scientific fact
As the density threshold changes, the counts of clusters, loops, and voids fluctuate like a heartbeat. Betti curves record this variation across scales.
The heartbeat of the universe is not a sound — it is an alternation of shapes.
Each time the density drops, a generation of clusters dies and a generation of voids is born.
A Betti curve is not mathematics — it is the universe counting the holes in itself.
06
Particles · Neutrinos
The invisible authors
Scientific fact
Neutrinos have near-zero mass and barely interact with anything — yet through free-streaming they suppress small-scale structure and reshape the topology of the universe.
They are so light they barely have weight. Yet numerous enough to change the shape of everything.
Invisible particles, in invisible places, rewriting all that can be seen.
Neutrinos are not the protagonists — but they wrote the script the protagonists follow.
05
Particles · Voids as information
The secret lives in the empty
Scientific fact
The structures most sensitive to neutrino mass are not galaxy clusters — they are voids. The topology of voids encodes the neutrino signal most clearly.
You spent a lifetime staring at the stars — but the answer was hiding in the space between them.
A void is not nothing. It is where the universe keeps its secrets.
The largest structure is the quietest one.
15
Particles · Suppression effect
Voids broken into pieces
Scientific fact
Heavier neutrinos make voids smaller, shallower, and more numerous — large voids fragment into smaller ones as density distributions contract toward the mean.
Heavy particles crowd the voids. What was once vast open space gets cut into pieces.
The voids did not disappear — they became fragmented, shallower, no longer alone.
13
Particles · Matter fields
Skeleton and flesh
Scientific fact
The cold dark matter field (excluding neutrinos) constrains neutrino mass weakly; the total matter field (including neutrinos) constrains it more strongly. The difference comes from a fraction effect, not a position effect.
The dark matter field is a map that records only the skeleton. The total matter field is the flesh.
Neutrinos take up no space — yet their presence changes the scale of the entire map.
07
Observation · Redshift distortion
The crooked universe
Scientific fact
Because galaxies are moving, their observed positions are stretched or compressed. The universe we observe and the universe that exists are related by a distortion.
You thought you were looking at the universe. You were looking at its reflection — stretched by velocity, flattened by direction.
In redshift space, everything is crooked. But inside the crooked lies the true.
